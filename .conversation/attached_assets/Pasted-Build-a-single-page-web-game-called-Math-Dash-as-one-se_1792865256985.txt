Build a single-page web game called Math Dash as one self-contained index.html file (HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript, no external libraries). It should run in a browser with no setup.

Core gameplay

The player is a small rounded square that moves through a side-scrolling level, in the style of a classic platformer like Super Mario. The camera follows the player, and the level has ground, gaps, and raised platforms.
Controls
Left and Right arrows (or A and D) move the square. Movement has acceleration and a little friction, so it feels responsive but not floaty.
Space jumps. Holding Space jumps higher, and releasing early cuts the jump short, as in Mario. The square can jump only when it’s on the ground.
Down arrow (or S) ducks and flattens the square.
Touch: on-screen left, right, and jump buttons, drawn as minimal translucent circles.
Falling into a gap ends the run.
Math gates are placed along the level. Each gate is a pair of answer blocks on platforms with a question floating above them, such as “7 × 8 = ?”. Land on the block with the correct answer to pass. Landing on a wrong block or touching a hazard ends the run.
Hazards are simple spikes or blocks. Avoid them by jumping or ducking.
Difficulty increases with distance: gates get harder, hazards get more frequent, and gaps get wider.

The player’s face (emotion)

Two simple dot eyes and a small curved mouth drawn with flat shapes.
Neutral while moving, wide eyes while jumping, happy arcs on a correct answer, X eyes and a frown on a crash, squinting while ducking.
Transition between expressions with a short, smooth morph.

Difficulty tiers (by distance)

0 to 500m: single-digit addition and subtraction
500 to 1500m: times tables up to 12 and simple division
1500 to 3000m: two-digit by one-digit multiplication, squares, and negative numbers
3000m and beyond: mixed operations, square roots, and percentages

Level sections

Every 1000m, the palette fades to a new one: warm off-white with charcoal (day), soft slate blue with pale gray (dusk), and deep navy with muted teal accents (night). The square, face, and answer blocks use the accent color.
Every 2000m, a boss gate appears: a large simple shape blocks the path with a two-step problem. The player has 3 attempts. Each wrong attempt shrinks the boss. Solving it grants bonus points.

Power-ups (appear rarely as small floating shapes)

Hint: softly outlines the correct answer block for 3 seconds
Slow motion: eases movement speed down for 5 seconds

Scoring

Score = distance (in meters) + 10 points per correct answer. Show the current score and best score (saved with localStorage in a try/catch).

Screens

Start screen: the title, a one-line control hint, and “Press Space to start” in small type.
Game over screen: final score, distance, best score, and “Press Space to retry.”

Visual style and motion

Render with Canvas. Use flat shapes, thin lines, generous whitespace, and one accent color per palette. No gradients or drop shadows.
Use a sans-serif font with clear numerals. Keep text small and quiet.
Animate with easing. Jumps use an ease-out on the way up and ease-in on the way down. Answer blocks fade and scale in gently. Palettes cross-fade.
On crash, the square briefly fades and the screen flashes softly. On a correct answer, use a small, quiet ripple.
Use requestAnimationFrame with delta time and target 60fps.
Restarting should take less than a second.

Structure

Organize the code into labeled sections: config, input, level generation, physics and collision, rendering, face, and UI.

Make sure the game works with keyboard on desktop and with the on-screen buttons on mobile. Verify that jumping, variable jump height, collision, answer detection, face expressions, palette transitions, and difficulty tiers all work before finishing.
